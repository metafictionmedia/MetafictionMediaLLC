About Metafiction Media

Our Mission

We create stories that are emotionally honest, creatively daring, and narratively sophisticated. Our meta-narrative approach amplifies emotional impact by acknowledging the artificial nature of storytelling while celebrating its power to create human connection.

Every project respects audience intelligence, delivering genuine experiences across age groups and media formats.

Creative Vision

We bridge innovative storytelling techniques with universal emotional experiences, building a scalable creative universe that expands across multiple media while maintaining consistent depth.

Our approach creates deeper engagement and more memorable experiences through self-aware narratives.

Meet the Creator

Founder & Creative Director

A visionary storyteller passionate about creating narratives that break the fourth wall while maintaining emotional authenticity. With a background in creative writing and digital media, the founder brings a unique perspective to children's literature and interactive storytelling.

Driven by the belief that audiences of all ages deserve sophisticated, honest content, the creative approach combines traditional storytelling with innovative meta-narrative techniques. This philosophy extends across all Metafiction Media projects, from books to digital experiences.

The journey began with a simple question: What if characters knew they were in stories? This curiosity has evolved into a comprehensive creative universe that challenges conventional narrative boundaries while celebrating the power of imagination and connection.

The Meta-Narrative Advantage

Characters acknowledge they're in stories, plots reference their own structure, and emotional moments feel genuine because they don't hide behind narrative conventions.

Jeffree the Monster

Where Self-Awareness Meets Heart

Story

Target: Ages 6-12 with cross-generational appeal

Themes: Identity, acceptance, emotional intelligence

On the first day of eighth grade, Jeffree the Monster just wants to survive without being noticed. But after getting humiliated on the way to school—and stuck with the only kid who calls him a friend, Barry—he finds out they're both being forced to perform in the school talent show by the end of the week. Can two outcasts pull it off, or will the pressure crush them before they hit the stage?

This self-awareness deepens rather than diminishes emotional impact, allowing honest conversations about real feelings and experiences.
